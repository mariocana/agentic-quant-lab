Why this comes before any tool.

The mindset decides whether the tool ever works for you.

Before we look at a single footprint chart — before we talk about bid and ask, delta and absorption — we have to spend time on the things that actually decide whether you make it in this job. You can have the most powerful tool in trading, and if your mindset is wrong, you'll still lose money.

This material has four objectives. If even one of them sticks, we've been successful and the time was worth it.

"It's a binary outcome. Price can only go up or down. We make it complicated the minute we start inviting information — blogs, Twitter, the guy on Bloomberg, useless indicators. The minute we invite it in, we complicate a binary outcome."

"I spent the first three or four years of my career being that guy who desperately wanted to be right. I wanted to hold it all day, tell everyone I made 100 points. I wanted to rub that ego. Then the mindset changed — from wanting to be right, to being right at the right time."

If even one of these sticks, it was worth your time.

These four are the whole game. You don't need to walk out with all of them. Just one — really understood — changes how you trade on Monday.

01

Objectivity

How do I know I'm right? How do I know this trade is right? How do I know my process is right? Objectivity is being able to look at your own work and challenge it, not just defend it. Most traders defend their losing trades. The professional asks: what evidence would change my mind?

02

Risk management

Where am I wrong? When am I wrong? Most traders focus on entries. Professionals focus on where the trade is invalidated and what they will do when it is.

03

Leverage

You don't need to be right in this job. You need to be right at the right time. Right place, right size, right moment — and do it with leverage. This is what the spread betters and the fancy brokerages tell you, and they're correct.

04

The information spectrum

What is the ultimate price of information? Price, in essence, should always be correctly priced if all information is accurate. If we know that — how can we throw ourselves into the idea that we have information other people don't?

PART 1 · OVERVIEW
07 / 49
BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

A squawk on the floor. And then a computer.

The footprint didn't start as a chart. It started as a voice yelling actual volumes across a trading pit.

Back in the pit days, when someone bought 1,000 contracts at 97, a guy on the floor shouted it into a squawk box. That was how you knew what actually traded — not the bid, not the offer, the real fill. Traders built their read of the market from those shouts.

When the pits closed, the squawk went with them. Someone asked: can a computer do this? By 2002 the concept was born. By 2012 professionals were adopting it. The footprint is the squawk, automated.

"Someone on the floor said 'the net positioning is short' and pointed at this colourful chart. I wrote it down. Price went lower. Next day he said it again. Same chart. Same outcome."

"I thought — hang on. This guy's making money. Maybe he found the holy grail. So I started looking at it. And I never stopped."

PART 1 · WORKFLOW
14 / 49

Eight reasons the footprint is worth your time.

  1. 01

    Funnels noise into information

    Most traders have lots of little things on their charts — moving averages, volume indicators, all sorts of things. The footprint takes all of that information — everything going on — and puts it in one graphic.

  2. 02

    Extracts the most relevant information

    Price, time, actual volume, relative market changes, and the interaction between buyers and sellers — all in one view. A much better depiction of what's actually going on.

  3. 03

    Replaces the reliance conundrum

    The reliance conundrum: you've got a line on the candlestick, and you buy because the candlestick shows a line there. We rely on the tool to make the decision. The footprint flips that. You're reliant on the buyers and sellers — not the tool.

  4. 04

    Objective rather than subjective

    The moment you have physical volume involved — someone bought, then bought the next price, then bought the next — we don't need to argue about what happened. We can see it. Someone bought every single price. Therefore there's a buyer. Therefore we have support.

  5. 05

    Leading rather than lagging

    The footprint shows you what's happening as it happens, not after the fact. The moment information enters the market, the footprint reveals it.

  6. 06

    Standalone or in conjunction

    It can be used as a standalone tool — you don't need other tools. Or in conjunction with candlesticks and market profile. Both approaches work. The professional workflow typically combines all three.

  7. 07

    Perfects market timing & risk management

    Ideal for leveraged trading. The earliness of the signal combined with the precision of the read means you can size accurately and stop precisely. This is what makes the footprint a professional tool.

  8. 08

    The relative indicator — the master concept

    This must be highlighted. It shows you relative changes. Nuanced shifts. Anything you need to know in the market on a relative perspective. It's not the absolute numbers — it's how the volume, delta, and interaction are changing from rotation to rotation. That's where the signal lives.

2.1

Software providers

Pick your partner, not just your tool.

The footprint is an expensive tool — in money, in time, in muscle memory. ATAS, NinjaTrader, Bookmap, Sierra Chart, DeepCharts — every one of them does some things well, some things badly. None of them is objectively best.

A lot of this is personal. What feels natural in your hand. Which workflows match how you think. Which data feed your broker supports. You're not buying a tool — you're picking a partner you'll spend thousands of hours with.

Once you've spent six months building your stats table, memorising your colour rules, learning which keystrokes do what — you're not going anywhere. The day someone tells you to "just try this other platform, it's better," you tell them to piss off. And rightly so. The cost of switching is enormous.

So pay for the right information the first time. Demo at least two before you commit. The platform you'll still be using in five years is the one whose limitations you can live with — not the one with the longest feature list.

2.2

Time-frames & rotations

The tool serves the question.

There is no objectively correct timeframe. There is only the one that matches what you're trying to read. A scalper and a swing trader can both use the footprint — but they should never use it the same way.

When I first traded Bitcoin, we were doing 450 contracts a day. A 5-minute footprint was empty — there was literally nothing in it. I used daily footprints. One question: where are buyers providing liquidity? That was the whole strategy.

The mistake is copying someone else's setup and wondering why you can't see what they see. What's your question? Pick the timeframe that answers it.

And then there's the other half of the decision — how each bar starts and ends.

A rotation is just a bar — but the rule you pick for when one bar closes and the next begins changes what the footprint is even telling you. Same prints, sorted differently, becomes a different story.

Time-based. Five minutes, sixty, a day. Consistent blocks I can compare across — same window, same question, every bar. That's how pattern recognition gets built.

Range, volume, or custom. The bar closes when price moves a fixed amount, when a fixed number of contracts trade, or by some other rule you define (1% rotations for equity swing, anything that sorts your data into something readable). Range normalises for volatility; volume puts every cell on the same activity footing.

I knew a guy who traded only volume bars in the bunds. He'd watched the same chart so long that when a single bar took twice as long to close as the one before it, he knew the auction had stalled before the tape did. Same volume, more time to put it through. The bar was telling him something the clock would have hidden.

Once you pick, stick with it. Every time you change the rotation, you're asking a different question — and you'll never learn the answer to a question you're not asking twice.

2.4

Statistics to include

Add it because it decides something.

Most platforms give you a summary row under each rotation — total volume, total delta, cumulative delta, price change. They exist so you don't have to do the addition in your head while the market is moving.

That's the whole rule: add a statistic because it shortens the path to a decision. Not because it looks complete. Not because you saw it on someone else's chart. If you can't say which decision it changes, it's noise.

If you're adding something, it means you don't know what your process is. Pick your process first. Then the stats table builds itself.

Indicators

Two questions, every time.

For every indicator you're about to put on the chart, ask these two questions:

Does this really help me? And — does it give me information I don't already have? — The indicator filter

If you've got an indicator telling you something another tool already shows, you're not adding signal — you're adding noise.

Moving average. I use one. Not to buy off the line, not for crossovers. Just for the slope. If I'm long and the footprint says buyers are in, a sloping-up MA is one extra confirmation in my peripheral vision. That's it.

VWAP. Add it if you actually trade off it. If you're just "looking at where VWAP is," it's a coin you never spend — take it off.

Volume profile. Same rule. If your strategy uses it, keep it. If you keep glancing past it, it's noise dressed as analysis.

The chart you can read in one second is more valuable than the chart that tells you everything you might possibly want to know.

Foundations

BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

Two lenses you should already own.

Everything that follows is read through these two lenses. We're not going to teach them — we're going to assume you've handled them before. Consider this the cover of a book you've already read.

LENS 01

Auction Market Theory

The market is a never-ending auction in search of fair price.

It only ever does two things: balance (buyers and sellers agree, range builds) and discovery (one side wins, price travels). Everything we'll talk about is one of those two states — or the moment one becomes the other.

BALANCE
Range. Two-way trade. Acceptance.
DISCOVERY
Trend. One-sided. Rejection.
LENS 02

Volume Spread Analysis

Three numbers, one question: is the effort matching the result?

VSA reads every bar as a relationship between spread (range), volume (effort), and close (outcome). When effort and outcome agree, the move is real. When they disagree — that's the signal something else is going on underneath.

SPREAD
How far it moved
VOLUME
What it cost
CLOSE
Who actually won
"If either of these lenses feels new — pause here. Read Dalton on AMT, Wyckoff or Williams on VSA. The footprint isn't a replacement for theory — it's the microscope you put on top of it."

The auction explained

BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

It's just an art auction.

A gallery offloading a painting. A room of collectors who want it. The auctioneer in the middle. Every financial market — at its root — is exactly this.

The gallery

Sellers

Holding inventory they need to move. If no hand goes up, they lower the reserve — and keep lowering — until someone bites.

The collectors

Buyers

They came to leave with the painting. If the gallery refuses to drop the price, they raise their paddle — and keep raising — until somebody lets go.

A buyer makes price go up— so long as no one sells to them.
A seller makes price go down— so long as no one buys from them.

PART 3 · STORY
22 / 49
BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

A morning, told as a story.

No prediction. Just narration — what the buyers did, what the sellers did, and what changed. This is the debrief you should be doing every morning.

01

Market opens. Sellers come in.

Big price ranges. Prices drop quickly. But — the delta doesn't go negative. What does that tell us? If sellers are hitting and delta isn't going red, the buyers aren't providing liquidity yet. Sellers are pushing into thin air.

02

Volume picks up. Delta goes negative. Price stops.

Interesting. Lots of selling, lots of effort — and price doesn't change. Someone is sitting there absorbing. The story just shifted. There's a buyer in the market now. We don't know who. We don't need to know.

03

Price ticks up. Slowly at first.

The absorber is being rewarded. Effort is finally getting price. This is the conclusive close — the moment the market confirms that all that buying is finally winning.

04

Rotation rips. Imbalances skip over prices.

Right back to the high. No one's selling on the way up. Effortless. Now we know who was on the other side — the sellers from rotation 01 are now the trapped ones.

"Tell yourself a story. Every morning. Out loud. 'This is my story — I don't need you to agree with me.' So long as you keep observing it and executing on it, the process stays consistent. Outcomes won't always be profitable. They will be consistent. That's the game."

02

Overlap of rotations vs. one-time framing

BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

Overlapping rotations — price isn't going anywhere.

Each new rotation overlaps the last. The auction is making no progress in either direction. This is not the market to be aggressive in.

Energy without resolution.

When rotations overlap, the buyers and sellers are roughly balanced. Nobody is in control. Volume might be there. Delta might be flipping. But no side is winning the auction.

This is where most traders force trades. They see activity and confuse it with opportunity. They're not the same. Activity without resolution is just noise.

The footprint warning sign: cumulative delta drifts, no rotation closes meaningfully past the prior. Wait for the auction to choose a direction.

PART 3 · STRONG AUCTION
24 / 49
BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

The market isn't wrong. You read it wrong.

The reason most traders blow up on one-time framing setups isn't bad luck — it's reading the shape without checking the volume. Here's the trap, and the only way out of it.

The pattern is seductive. You see one-time framing down — aggressive, push push push — and your instinct is to wait for the first pullback and short it. The market then gives everyone a chance to get short. And then it straight-line reverses the other way.

Your first thought: "the market is wrong." It isn't. The market is never wrong. You missed the read.

Look closer at the footprint of that "aggressive" down move. Lots of zeros. Pretty thin volume. No real negative delta. Nobody was actually selling — price was just travelling through empty air. There was no fuel under the move. The first real buyers showed up and the whole thing unwound.

Relative change — the master concept

BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

Not the absolute number — the change.

Relative change is the single most important thing the footprint shows you. Not the number. How the number is changing.

The shift IS the signal.

2,000 contracts of delta means nothing in isolation. 2,000 contracts when the prior rotation was 200 — that's a 10× shift. That's a signal. The footprint doesn't give you absolute truth. It gives you change, rotation to rotation, and you read that change like a vital sign.

Five things to watch change in — every rotation:

  • → Price change (range expanding or contracting?)
  • → Volume change (growing or fading?)
  • → Delta change (flipping or sustaining?)
  • → Cumulative delta change (positioning building or unwinding?)
  • → Interaction change (absorbing now vs hitting through?)

Traditional tools show one of these at a time — volume on a bar chart, delta on an oscillator. The footprint shows all five in one frame. That's why the shift is visible here that's invisible elsewhere.

EXAMPLE · ALL FIVE AT ONCE

Effort going up. Volume going up. Delta going up. Price doing nothing. No single number means anything. But the shift — sellers got bigger, price stopped responding — that's a relative change signal. Someone is absorbing. The trade lives in that one observation.

WHY ONE WEEK FEELS LIKE NOISE

Relative change isn't a single moment — it's a habit of comparison. The pattern only emerges after you've watched enough rotations to know how shifts usually play out. This is why people who've used the footprint for a week see noise, and people who've used it for years see clearly. It's not a smarter tool — it's a more patient observer.

Two completely different characters.

Technical

  • Price rotating back into value area
  • No expansion in volatility
Great for absorption setups and range fades.

4.6

Positioning

BLOOD · SWEAT · SCALP

The fuel that turns a bad extreme into a trade.

Who's trapped — and how violently they'll have to get out.

Positioning is the aggregate of what every participant currently holds. Cumulative delta gives you the read. Net short means the market is collectively short — and every short has to be covered eventually. When price rises against them, the pain compounds. They're forced to buy back. Forced covering ADDS to buying pressure. More buying → higher price → more pain → more covering. The move self-reinforces.

This is why short squeezes are so violent — they cascade. Long liquidations into net-long markets cascade the same way downward. A bad extreme without positioning behind it is just a curiosity. A bad extreme WITH positioning is a setup with built-in fuel.

Bad low + net short positioning
= highest probability long setup in orderflow.
Bad high + net long positioning
= highest probability short setup.

Every loss is a receipt.

Oil, three weeks straight. Lost on every trade. Every single one. Reluctant sellers at the lows, reluctant buyers at the highs — the footprint showed it. And we kept fighting it anyway. That's gambling. Not following a process. Say it out loud: gambling.

But here's the thing — every losing trade was teaching the shape of that reluctance. What the absorption looked like. How the delta behaved. When the buyers gave up vs when they doubled down. That's paying for information.

The day that reluctance shifts — when sellers finally stop being reluctant and start being aggressive — you'll see it before anyone else. Because you've been staring at it for three weeks. You hit that trade hard enough to get paid back for every frustrating loss you took learning to recognise it.

"Don't lose the lesson. The lesson is paid for in losses — and it's only worth what you paid if you actually go back and look at why."

The footprint is not the holy grail.

Nothing in trading is. The footprint is a tool — the most powerful tool I know for reading the real-time battle between buyers and sellers. But without discipline, without a process, without honesty about when you're wrong — it's just an expensive chart.

The discipline comes from the debrief. The process comes from these four parts: understand the tool, set it up correctly, apply the buying and selling principles, recognise the market structures. The honesty comes from being willing to say: I was wrong, here's why, here's what I look for next time.

"You don't need to be right in this job. You need to be right at the right time, with the right size, consistently. The footprint gives you the earliest and most objective signal of when that right time is. The rest is process, discipline, and the willingness to keep coming back and learning."
